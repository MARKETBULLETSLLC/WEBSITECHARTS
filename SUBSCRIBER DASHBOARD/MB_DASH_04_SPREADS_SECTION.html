SRW — Chicago Soft Red Winter
CBOT
The benchmark U.S. wheat contract and the most liquid grain futures market in the world. Delivery in Chicago; settles against soft red winter wheat — the class used for cookies, crackers, and pastry flour. Primary hedging vehicle for eastern wheat belt producers and the global reference price for wheat.

HRW — Kansas City Hard Red Winter
KCBT
The Plains wheat contract. Hard red winter wheat is the bread flour class — higher protein, grown across Kansas, Oklahoma, Texas panhandle, Colorado, and Nebraska. The Kansas City contract is the primary hedging vehicle for the largest U.S. wheat-producing region and a key indicator of domestic milling demand and export competitiveness.

HRS — Minneapolis Hard Red Spring
MGEX
Premium milling wheat grown in the northern tier — Montana, North Dakota, South Dakota, and Minnesota. Highest protein content of the major U.S. wheat classes, used for premium bread flour, bagels, and high-gluten applications. Typically commands a premium over both SRW and HRW; the HRS/SRW spread is a closely watched protein premium indicator.

FWH — Paris Milling Wheat
Euronext
The European milling wheat benchmark, quoted in euros per metric tonne. Reflects EU crop conditions, Black Sea competition, and global export dynamics. A critical reference for North African and Middle Eastern import demand — the same buyers who compete with U.S. wheat on the world market. The FWH/SRW spread is a key gauge of U.S. export competitiveness.

Inter-Market Spreads & Ratios
Cross-Exchange
Price relationships between wheat classes and between wheat and other grains. These spreads reveal relative demand for protein, the competitiveness of individual growing regions, and structural shifts in end-user preferences. The wheat/corn ratio is a key input into livestock and ethanol demand models.
