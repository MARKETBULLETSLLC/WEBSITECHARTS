Stop watching the board. Start working the plan.

Most of us don't check prices four times a day because something changed. We check because something might have. The box checks once, at settlement, so you don't have to keep checking. You can put the phone down because you already know where things stand, not because you've given up.

What's in the box, and what it does for you

Two states: Suspend or Execute.

You know your posture before the coffee goes cold. With fewer calls to make, there are fewer to second-guess at supper.

It reads settlement, not the noise.

Midday headlines come and go like a spring squall. The box waits for the close, and that gives you permission to wait too.

The Tripwire: an early call to attention.

You get time to take the plan out of the drawer before the market forces you to. Nobody ever lost a farm by being ready a day early.

The Threshold: the line that matters.

Decide in calm what you'll do in the storm. Write the order on a quiet Tuesday so you aren't writing it on a panicked Thursday. Measure twice, sell once.

A public, dated record.

You can check our work, right calls and wrong ones. Trust builds the way topsoil does: slowly, one season at a time. It can be lost in one windy afternoon, so we keep the record where you can see it.

It doesn't work alone.

The box sits inside The Warm Signal, so export pace, fund positioning from the CFTC's Commitments of Traders report, and seasonal patterns stand behind every reading. The Black Sea harvest, the dollar and the funds all leave their mark on Chicago settlement. The box reads where they land.

Conservative by temperament

We're conservative about two things: spending money we haven't made, and pricing bushels we haven't thought through.

A penny saved is a penny earned. A bushel priced in a panic is neither.

Old-fashioned? Guilty. So are fence posts, and they still keep the cattle where they belong.

Not just for the kitchen table

Merchandisers use the box to read what producers are likely to do next. Lenders use it to ask better questions at renewal. Every link in the chain reads the same line at the same hour, before sunrise.

The operating loan doesn't care how hard the crop was to grow. It cares what you sold it for. Box-o-Rox won't make that call for you, and it shouldn't. It makes sure you don't make it alone, half-awake, on a rumor. The boots get smaller. The decisions get bigger. Make yours with a plan.

Get the Box Every Morning

The Rules of the Rocks

Two lines. Two states. One question every morning: are we waiting, or are we working?

Box-o-Rox reads where Chicago soft red winter wheat settles against two lines we draw every day.

The Threshold is the slow line. It moves about as fast as a banker approving a line of credit.

The Tripwire is the quick line. It moves about as fast as news travels from the café to the shop.

How either line is built stays in the box. That part is proprietary. Like Grandpa's best fishing hole, it isn't on any map.

The two states

SUSPEND.Settlement holds above the Threshold, and the market is accumulating. The posture is patience. Keep your powder dry, leave the bushels you planned to hold unpriced, and let the market come to you.

EXECUTE (Tripwire):the early call. Settlement is still above the Threshold but breaks below the Tripwire, with no discretionary hold in place. It isn't a fire alarm yet; it's the smell of smoke. Take the marketing plan out of the drawer and check which bushels are next in line.

EXECUTE (Threshold):the main call. Settlement drops below the Threshold, and the market is in its execution phase. This is the time to price, sell, or put protection in place, following the plan you wrote while the market was quiet.

When settlement climbs back above both lines, the box returns to SUSPEND.

The fine print (printed large on purpose)

  • Settlement, not the midday tape. The box reads the close. It doesn't flinch at a lunchtime headline, and your plan shouldn't either.
  • One state at a time. The box is always in exactly one state. There's no "leaning," no "sort of," no "we'll see."
  • The discretionary hold. Sometimes the wider read argues against an early call: export pace, fund positioning, the seasonal pattern. Then a hold can be placed, and it's marked on the chart where everyone can see it. A hold can delay an early call. It never overrides a confirmed break below the Threshold.
  • The record stays on the board. Every change of state is dated and logged, right calls and wrong ones alike. We don't erase the chalkboard.
  • The box informs. You decide. It doesn't know your basis, your bin space or your banker. You do.

Why rocks?

A good signal ought to be dumber than a box of them. Rocks don't panic, don't read headlines, and have never called the elevator at two in the morning. Everything clever happens before the line gets drawn. After that, it's a rock: above the line or below it.
