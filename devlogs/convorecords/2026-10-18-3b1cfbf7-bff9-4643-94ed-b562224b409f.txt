This is a Trade Navigator job, not a code change. Trade Navigator has no built-in "fill under a line" setting that I know of. The workaround is to add a second copy of the MA60 and draw it as a pale histogram instead of a line. A histogram fills straight down from the line to the bottom of the chart, which gives you the shading.

I haven't tested this on your copy of Trade Navigator. The menu names below come from the 2011â€“2013 manuals, so some labels may have changed.

### 1. Shade the area under the MA60

1. Click the BoR chart so it's selected, then press **A** to open the add-indicators window.
2. Add the same moving average you already use for the green line: average of open/high/low/close, 60 days. You now have two copies of it.
3. Press **E** for chart settings and select the new copy.
   - **Plot type:** Histogram (instead of Line)
   - **Color:** a soft tint, something like RGB 215, 235, 205. Trade Navigator probably can't make colors see-through, so the solid color itself has to be light.
   - **Bar width:** as wide as it goes, so the bars touch and look like one solid area.
   - **Scale:** the same scale and panel as the price bars, so it doesn't get its own scale.
4. In the list of items on the chart, move the histogram so it's drawn **behind** the candles and both average lines.

**Check this right away:** a histogram starts at zero. If Trade Navigator stretches the price scale down to zero to fit it, the candles get squashed into a thin strip. If that happens, look for a setting that stops the histogram from changing the scale. If you can't find one, tell me what you see and we'll pick a different approach.

### 2. Add the "Execute Sale of Tranche(s)" label

Use the same text tool you used for "Threshold" and "Tripwire." Type **Execute Sale of Tranche(s)** in bold, about the same size as "Tripwire" or a bit bigger, in dark green so it stands out on the pale shading.

The best open space inside the shaded area is between about **07/27 and 09/07, around 600â€“615**. That spot stays clear of the candles and of the text box over 09/21â€“10/19.

**One catch:** text in Trade Navigator is pinned to a date and price. As new days come in, the label slides left and eventually drops off the chart, so you'd need to move it every few weeks. To avoid that, you could rename the histogram itself to "Execute Sale of Tranche(s)." The name then shows in the top line next to the MA20 and MA60 readings, in the shading's color, and never moves. It's less obvious than a label inside the shading, but it needs no upkeep.

### Two other things I noticed
- **Placeholder text is still on the chart.** The blue "Lorem ipsumâ€¦" text near 10/05 should be replaced or removed before this chart goes to a public or members' page.
- **The shading only covers the 60-day signal.** Below the 60-day line is where EXECUTE (60-D) applies. The 20-day early signal fires above the green line, in the gap between the two lines when the red one is on top. Right now the red line is below the green one, so that gap doesn't exist yet. When it does, a second, even lighter shading there with its own label would show both signals.

## Two separate pieces, not one skill
1. **The drawing engine.** This is a Python script like the test chart. It takes price data plus an annotations file and draws the chart. It runs on its own, triggered automatically, with no Claude involved. This is what makes it automated.
2. **The skill.** This is Claude's side of the job. You paste rough data and notes, and Claude turns them into a clean annotations file, runs the engine, and shows you a preview. It's for days when you're adding judgment calls, not for routine updates.

If a skill were the only way to make charts, every chart would need a Claude session during your publishing windows. That would be a new daily dependency, which the automation rules don't allow. So the engine carries the daily load and the skill is the convenient way to edit.
