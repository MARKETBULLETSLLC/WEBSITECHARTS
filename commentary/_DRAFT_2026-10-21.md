Monday morning we scouted the terrain and named the next real floor: the Aug. 11 swing low at $6.45¼, where the August rally lifted off. Tuesday the market walked up to that floor, leaned on it, and went through. Dec Chicago settled at $6.43¼, down 12¢ on the day and 17¼¢ in two sessions. KC and Minneapolis went with it, and [ADM Investor Services](https://www.admis.com/ag-market-view-for-oct-20-2026/) has all three classes at fresh two-to-three-month lows. Here's why that matters more than another red number. The August low was the last level on the daily chart that let you call the whole Black Sea run a rally off a base. With it gone, the base is behind us too. Seven weeks ago the question was how high the war could carry wheat. Today the question is what's left underneath it.

**How it broke matters more than where.** Tuesday didn't open weak. Dec opened at $6.56 and worked up near $6.62. That was the bounce oversold markets are supposed to produce, maybe with a few shorts covering. Then the selling found it, and Dec closed 19¢ off the high, near the low of the day. A market that can't hold a morning rally is telling you the sellers weren't finished. They were waiting for a better price, and the bounce gave them one. On Monday we said a pop toward the Tripwire would be a gift to an EXECUTE plan. Tuesday the pop showed up for a couple of hours and never got within 20¢ of the Tripwire. That isn't a reason for regret. It's the reason scale-up offers get written down ahead of time and left resting. Bounces off oversold lows don't send invitations, and when they do show up, they don't stay for supper. *[EDITOR: historical parallel, an October support break from past years and how the following weeks played out]*

**Scout the terrain again.** The floor that broke becomes the first ceiling. On any recovery, $6.45¼ is now the level the market has to reclaim, and it'll be tested from underneath. Below Tuesday's low, there's not much on the daily until the summer lows. *[EDITOR: confirm the July low on the Dec chart]* The weekly retracement of the whole October-2025-to-September run still sits at $6.06¼. None of that is a forecast. It's a map, so nobody has to draw one at 7:29 a.m.

**The market has gone deaf in one ear.** Monday brought renewed strikes on vessels in the Black Sea, and wheat fell anyway, to its lowest since Aug. 12, per Bloomberg via [UkrAgroConsult](https://ukragroconsult.com/en/news/wheat-market-gradually-becomes-less-responsive-to-black-sea-export-disruptions/). The disruption itself is real, not imagined. Both sides of the war report an export pace more than 50% behind a year ago, per [the Price Group](https://blog.pricegroup.com/2026/10/20/grains-report-10-20-2026/), and the [Grain Central](https://www.graincentral.com/) wire, as summarized by [CMB News](https://commodity-board.com/wheat-caught-between-black-sea-supply-shock-and-demand-headwinds), reported no wheat leaving Russia's Black Sea ports since late September. Yet the headlines the trade is listening to are on the peace side. Turkey has floated [two separate safe corridors](https://ukragroconsult.com/en/news/turkey-proposes-separate-safe-corridors-for-ukrainian-and-russian-vessels-in-the-black-sea/), one for each side's ships, and ADM reports the EU working with Ukraine, Turkey, India and North African importers to press for a safe-navigation agreement and bring Russia to the table. The market hears the talks and tunes out the gunfire. The reason is the one we laid out Monday: unavailable isn't the same as gone. Importers are drawing down their own stocks and buying from other origins while Russia's grain sits in its piles. The ear that's closed is the one that would hear grain actually *lost*, not delayed. Nothing in the last two sessions qualified. That's a fact about this market today, not a law of nature, and it's worth knowing which ear to watch.

**Meanwhile, the U.S. isn't the alternative origin.** Export inspections for the week ending Oct. 15 came in at 240,308 tonnes, about 8.8 million bushels, per [the Price Group](https://blog.pricegroup.com/2026/10/20/grains-report-10-20-2026/). That's down from 307,025 the week before, and it's barely half the roughly 15.4 million a week it takes to reach USDA's 750-million-bushel export number. Marketing-year inspections stand at 7.22 MMT against 11.21 MMT a year ago, a 36% gap. ADM calls U.S. wheat "largely uncompetitive," and Jordan reportedly passed on both offers in its 120,000-tonne tender. With the dollar index steady near 102, there's no currency tailwind coming to the rescue. The world isn't short of wheat sellers. It's short of reasons to pick ours.

**At home, the drills are mostly parked.** Nationally, USDA had winter wheat 63% planted as of Sunday, up 12 points on the week but still behind the 69% average, with 39% emerged, also behind. Weather in the Plains has turned friendlier, with scattered showers in the forecast, per [the Price Group](https://blog.pricegroup.com/2026/10/20/grains-report-10-20-2026/). That takes some air out of a weather leg the market might otherwise have leaned on. Washington is running ahead of the country: 96% planted against a 90% average, and 80% emerged against 65%, with 6.5 days suitable for fieldwork last week, per [NASS via WAWG](https://wawg.org/updates/crop-progess-report-10-20/). *[EDITOR: local color, Prescott/Palouse seeding, moisture, how the stand looks]* For our home ground, the work in the field is about done for the year. The work at the phone isn't.

**The scissors are still open.** November WTI pushed to $103.66 Monday, a new high for the move, and went off the board Tuesday at $101.79, per [Brownfield](https://brownfieldagnews.com/news/closing-grain-and-livestock-futures-october-20-2026/). Diesel is north of $6 a gallon, and the [Farm Bureau told the White House](https://www.fb.org/files/Diesel-Letter_WH_10.1.26.pdf) earlier this month that on-highway diesel had reached $6.38. Tuesday's whole move fits on one line: wheat down 12¢, crude still over $100. The bid blade and the input blade kept cutting toward each other.

**Follow the spread to find the pressure.** KC's premium over Chicago has widened back to 47¾¢, from 43½¢ Friday. Last week hard red winter was taking the beating as KC funds liquidated longs. This week Chicago is leading the way down, and Chicago is where managed money was already short about 33,000 contracts as of Oct. 13, per the [CFTC](https://www.cftc.gov/dea/futures/ag_sf.htm). A short position pressing into a broken floor is moving with the wind at its back. Friday's report, with positions as of today, will show whether they pressed again. Inside Chicago, the Dec-to-March carry is about 15½¢, with March near $6.58¾. A carry that wide is the market offering to pay you to store wheat. It's offering to pay storage, though, not to guarantee the price.

Here's the part that doesn't fit in a bullet. In Washington, 80% of next year's crop is already up out of the ground. That seed is a promise made to next August, and nobody can take it back. The price of *this* crop is a different kind of promise, the kind you make on the phone, and you keep the right to decide it one tranche at a time. A floor breaking on a chart doesn't make the operation any less sound than it was Monday. It means the market has stopped paying for hope, and the producer who priced on plan was never selling hope in the first place. So before you pick up the phone this morning, find the line in your marketing plan that says what you do when support breaks. If it's there, follow it. If it isn't, write it this morning, before the next floor gets tested, not after.
