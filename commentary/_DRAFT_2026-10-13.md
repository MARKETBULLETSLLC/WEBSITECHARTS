# THE MAGAZINE IS EMPTY — AND THAT CHANGES THE MATH ON WAITING

- Chicago December settled 672¼ Monday, up 1¼, then slipped to 667½ overnight — 35 cents beneath the 60-day moving average (702½). Box-o-Rox remains in **EXECUTE (60-D)**, in effect since the September 28 close below 696¼.
- The 20-day average (697½) has now rolled under the 60-day (702½). The early warning and the main signal are both pointing the same way.
- USDA's [October WASDE](https://www.usda.gov/oce/commodity/wasde) raised U.S. 2026/27 ending stocks to 740 million bushels — up 23 million from September and 18 million above the trade guess. The build came from a 25-million-bushel **export cut** (to 750 million), not from a bigger crop. Season-average price was trimmed 10 cents to $6.30.
- USDA cut Russian exports to 40 MMT, a five-year low, on Black Sea logistics — and handed that business to Argentina and Canada, not the U.S. The Dollar Index sits at 102.15, up 3.79% year-to-date.
- CFTC Commitment of Traders (data as of Oct 6): the funds are net short only 16,048 SRW contracts. In May 2025 they were short 118,000. There is no crowded short position waiting to be covered.
- PNW soft white: the Portland bid was $6.65 for October with **+5¢/month carry through March** (Oct 5 quote). Five cents a month on $6.65 wheat works out to roughly 9% annualized. *[GARY: update to this morning's bid]*

---

One year ago this week, Chicago made its low: 492¼ on the weekly continuation chart, October 2025. From there, 51 weeks of higher lows carried it to 795 on September 2. Overnight, December printed 667½. The rally has now given back about 42% of what it gained, and the trendline that carried it for a year broke in late September. The anniversary is a fair moment to ask an honest question: is this a pullback inside an uptrend, or the end of the uptrend?

[chart: WHEAT_SRW_W | SRW weekly — one year off the Oct 2025 low at 492¼; the 51-week trendline is broken, and so is the 0.382 retracement at 676½]

Friday's WASDE gave a clear answer to part of that question. U.S. production came in at 1.534 billion bushels, a hair above September, and per [High Plains Journal's summary of the report](https://hpj.com/2026/10/12/october-9-wasde-report/) the smallest U.S. crop since 1970. Even so, stocks went **up**, because the customers didn't come. USDA cut exports 25 million bushels, and [hard red winter absorbed most of it](https://agoptimus.com/october-2026-wasde-actual-vs-estimates/): HRW stocks rose 14 million bushels while hard red spring actually tightened by 8 million. A short crop with a growing carryout is a demand problem, and demand problems don't fix themselves with a weather scare.

The global detail is where it gets interesting. USDA put Russian exports at 40 million metric tons, the lowest in five years, citing logistical disruption tied to the Black Sea conflict. In any other year, that would be the bullish headline of the month: the world's largest exporter leaving business on the table. USDA's own balance sheet says Argentina and Canada are filling the gap, not the Gulf or the Columbia River. The reason is on the dollar chart.

[chart: IDX_DXY_W | Dollar Index weekly — 102.15, above both the 66-week and 210-week trendlines; up 3.79% year-to-date]

The Dollar Index has broken above both of its long-term trendlines and is at its 2026 high, and the 2-year Treasury yield has climbed alongside it to 4.79%. Every point the dollar gains is a point off the competitiveness of every FOB offer out of Portland and the Gulf. Russia stepping back should have been America's opening. The exchange rate closed it.

[chart: WHEAT_SRW_BOR_D | Box-o-Rox — EXECUTE (60-D) since Sep 28; 20-day (697½) now beneath the 60-day (702½); price 35¢ below the benchmark]

Box-o-Rox has been in EXECUTE (60-D) for eleven sessions. A return to SUSPEND requires a settlement back above the 60-day, currently 702½, and that is 35 cents overhead. The 20-day crossing under the 60-day adds weight to the read: the short-term and intermediate averages have both turned over. On the downside, the levels Chicago respected on the way up are the August low at 626¾, the 0.618 weekly retracement at 606¼, and the June floor near 570.

Here's the part of the setup that most of the trade will gloss over. When wheat has fallen this hard before, there was usually a spring loaded underneath it: a big fund short position. In May 2025 the funds were short 118,000 SRW contracts. Any spark (a Black Sea headline, a crop scare, a dollar reversal) sent them scrambling to buy back, and the rally fed on itself. Today they're short 16,000. Commercials are net long 13,670. The magazine is nearly empty.

[chart: COT_SRW_W | SRW Commitment of Traders — funds net short just 16K vs. 118K in May 2025; no crowded short side to cover]

That doesn't mean wheat can't rally. It means a rally needs a **new reason**: fresh export demand, a weaker dollar, or a production problem somewhere in the Southern Hemisphere. It can't count on the funds' own exit to do the lifting. "It'll bounce when the funds cover" has been a reliable reason to wait in past years. This year, the numbers don't support it.

[chart: WHEAT_HRW_D | KC HRW daily — 713¾ overnight, below its BoR line at 761½ and its year-long trendline]

Kansas City tells the same story with an extra sentence attached. December KC is 713¾ overnight, below its own benchmark line (761½) and through the trendline from last October's low, and HRW is the class carrying most of USDA's export cut. Chicago and Kansas City, on the daily and the weekly, are all pointing the same direction. That is phase alignment. The signal is warm and clear. It just isn't warm in the direction anyone holding unpriced bushels wants it to be.

So what does a producer do with this at 7:30 this morning? The market is offering a trade-off, and it's worth pricing out in exact numbers. Portland was paying five cents a month to wait from October through March. On $6.65 wheat that's roughly 9% annualized, before storage, shrink, and insurance come out. Chicago's December-to-March spread pays around 13 to 14 cents over three months. The carry is the market's paid offer to hold the grain. **It is not paying you to hold the price risk.** A forward contract for deferred delivery (sell now, deliver in March) collects the carry without leaving the bushels exposed to the next leg of a move the benchmark already says is in effect.

For the merchandiser and the lender: HRW took 14 million bushels of the stock build. Watch whether that shows up in hard red winter basis and protein spreads before it shows up in the futures. And with Nov crude at $93.74 and USDA's season-average price now $6.30, the margin between what a bushel costs to raise and what it brings is thinner than paint on a used combine. An operating loan doesn't care which way the funds lean.

**Stance:** Box-o-Rox says execute, and nothing in Friday's report or this morning's tape argues otherwise. The usual reason to wait, a fund short-covering bounce, isn't loaded this year. The carry *is* paying, but only to those who price the grain and keep the bin. Before you call your elevator, three numbers belong on the same napkin: the carry on your contract, the rate on your operating note, and what your storage really costs. If the carry beats the other two, take the carry and price the bushels. If it doesn't, the bin is costing you money to hold a hope. Bushels priced on plan are bushels that can't be lost to hope. That is how a marketing plan protects the operation it was built for.
